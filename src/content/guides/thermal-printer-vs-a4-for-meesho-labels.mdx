This is the upgrade every Meesho seller considers at some point, usually while cutting the
fiftieth A4 sheet of the evening. It is worth being clear about what actually changes, because
the answer is not "thermal is better" — it is "thermal is better past a certain volume, and
before that it is money you did not need to spend".

## What is genuinely different

**Consumables.** A thermal printer uses no ink and no toner. Direct thermal printing works by
applying heat to coated label stock, so the only consumable is the roll. An inkjet or laser
printing on A4 uses ink or toner plus the paper plus the tape you use to attach it. Which works
out cheaper per label depends on current prices where you buy, so check them rather than trusting
any number in a blog post — including this one, which is why there isn't one.

**Durability.** A thermal label is adhesive stock designed to travel on the outside of a parcel.
Paper taped to a box is fine most of the time and not fine in monsoon. The known weakness of
direct thermal is heat and sunlight over time — a label can fade in storage — which is a real
issue for warehouse stock and a non-issue for a parcel that ships tomorrow.

**Speed and handling.** The thermal printer prints one label, already the right size, already
sticky. A4 means print, cut, tape. At ten orders that difference is nothing. At a hundred it is
most of your evening, and it is the cutting rather than the printing that costs the time.

**Failure modes.** Thermal printers jam and need the roll loaded the right way up — the coated
side faces the head, and a roll in backwards prints nothing at all while appearing to work
perfectly. Ordinary printers run out of ink at the worst moment. Neither is clearly better;
they just fail differently.

## Where A4 genuinely wins

- **You already own the printer.** Nothing to buy, nothing to learn.
- **You need the invoice too.** A4 holds a label and its invoice on one page, which suits sellers
  who file every invoice for GST.
- **Volume is low or irregular.** A machine that does one job, used twice a week, is a machine
  gathering dust.
- **You want to start today.** No delivery wait.

## Where thermal genuinely wins

- **Daily volume, every day.** The saved handling time compounds.
- **Parcels handled roughly or shipped in the wet season.**
- **You want the label to look professional.** It is a small thing, and customers do notice.
- **You have stopped wanting to cut paper.** Not a joke — this is the reason most sellers
  actually switch.

## A rough rule

If you are shipping under about ten orders a day, A4 four-per-sheet is a perfectly reasonable
setup and the money is better spent on stock. Somewhere in the tens per day the cutting and
taping starts to dominate your packing time, and that is the point at which a thermal printer
pays for itself in evenings rather than in rupees.

There is no need to guess in advance. Run on A4 until it annoys you; the annoyance is the signal.

## Either way, the file has to be right

Both paths need the tax invoice cropped off and the labels the right size — the difference is only
what "right size" means.

The [label cropper](/tools/meesho-label-cropper) outputs both. For thermal, it produces a PDF with
a true 4x6 or 3x5 inch page so the printer lays it down at 100% with no scaling. For A4, it packs
one, two or four cropped labels onto a sheet with a quarter-inch margin your printer can grip,
rotating each label to fill its cell.

If you do switch later, nothing about your process changes except one dropdown. More detail on
getting the print settings right is in the
[thermal printer guide](/guides/print-meesho-labels-on-thermal-printer); the common cause of
labels printing small with white edges is "fit to page" left on, not the file.
