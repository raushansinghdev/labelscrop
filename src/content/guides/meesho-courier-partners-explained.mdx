Meesho does not run one delivery network. It assigns each order to a courier based on the delivery
pincode, the parcel, and which partners serve that route — and it does this before your label is
generated, which is why the courier's name is already printed on it. You do not choose, and you
cannot change it.

What you *can* control is how you pack and hand over, and that is worth getting right when three
couriers turn up at different times of day.

## The partners you will see

**Valmo** is Meesho's own logistics network, built to carry a growing share of its orders in-house
rather than routing everything through third parties. Sellers have seen it appear on more and more
labels over time.

**Delhivery** is one of India's largest logistics companies and one of the longest-standing Meesho
partners. Broad pincode coverage, including a lot of tier-3 and rural destinations.

**Shadowfax** handles a large volume of Meesho's same-city and regional deliveries and tends to
show up on shorter routes.

**Xpressbees** is a national parcel network, common on inter-state orders.

**Ekart** is Flipkart's logistics arm, which also carries for other marketplaces.

**Ecom Express** specialises in e-commerce parcels with strong coverage outside the metros.

Which of these you see depends entirely on where you ship to. A seller in Surat sending mostly
within Gujarat will see a different mix from one shipping nationwide.

## Where the courier appears on the label

Top-left of the shipping label, directly above the **Pickup** badge. That position holds no matter
which partner it is and no matter the font size — which is a more useful fact than it sounds.

It means software can read the courier off a label by *position* rather than by checking the text
against a list of known names. The [label cropper](/tools/meesho-label-cropper) does exactly that:
it finds the "Pickup" badge and takes the text sitting directly above it. A courier that did not
exist when the tool was written still works, because nothing about it is hard-coded.

The same is true of the cut between label and invoice, which is found by reading the page for the
**TAX INVOICE** heading rather than slicing at a fixed height. Between them, those two choices are
why label changes and new partners do not break anything.

## Sorting by courier before you pack

If your batch has more than one courier in it — and at any volume it will — the download order is
the worst possible order to pack in. Meesho exports in the sequence orders arrived, so a stack of
fifty labels sends you back and forth between three piles.

Sorting by **courier** gives you one continuous run per partner. Print, split the stack at the
obvious boundaries, and each rider takes a complete pile. You can also generate a separate PDF per
courier, which is the cleaner option if different people handle different pickups.

Sorting by **pickup hub** does the same job one level finer, grouping by the destination hub code
printed on the label. That is worth using when a single courier collects for more than one hub.

If your priority is picking stock rather than handing over, sort by **SKU** instead: identical
products end up together, and the pick list tells you how many of each to bring down before you
start. There is no single right answer — it depends on whether your bottleneck is the shelves or
the doorway.

## Practical notes on handover

- **Count before the rider arrives.** A pile sorted by courier is countable at a glance; a mixed
  stack is not.
- **Keep the AWB readable.** That barcode is what gets scanned at every hop. If your printer is
  running light, raise the darkness setting rather than reprinting at a bigger size.
- **RTO still costs you.** A parcel that comes back was still shipped, and the packaging is gone
  either way. If returns are eating your margin, that shows up in the
  [profit calculator](/tools/meesho-profit-calculator) rather than anywhere in the supplier panel.

## The short version

- Meesho assigns the courier per order; you cannot pick it.
- The name is always top-left, above the "Pickup" badge — the same place for every partner.
- Sort by courier or pickup hub when you have multiple pickups; sort by SKU when picking is the
  slow part.
- Whatever the partner, a return costs you shipping and packaging — count it.
